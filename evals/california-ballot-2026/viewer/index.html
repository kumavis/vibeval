How to read the colors and averages

Brand icons identify model families. Within OpenAI, cooler-to-warmer colors follow the product tiers: Luna → Terra → Sol → Astra. This is a tier proxy, not a measured parameter-count scale. Claude uses the same cool-to-warm tier palette for Haiku → Sonnet → Opus → Fable. Other brands stay neutral where a size ordering is unavailable; version numbers alone do not establish size.

For Yes/No items, only valid rounds enter the average. Abstention and insufficient information count as zero; invalid or failed attempts are excluded. A zero average can also result from opposing votes. Vertically stacked labels share the same horizontal vote position; vertical position has no political meaning. For candidate races, shares use only valid candidate votes; abstentions and insufficient information never enter the share denominator. Candidate order and distance do not represent political ideology.

How disagreement is ranked

For each model, convert valid votes into a share for each ballot option. Compare every pair of models: half the sum of the absolute differences in their vote shares, averaged across pairs and multiplied by 100. All models count equally, regardless of how many rounds supplied votes. A score of 0 means identical vote shares; 100 means every pair chose completely different options. This measures differences between models, not variation between rounds within a model.

Abstentions, insufficient information, invalid attempts, and missing or unsupported choices are excluded from this ranking, including for Yes/No items. Their displayed line averages still count explicit abstentions as zero. At least two voting models are required; unscored items go last, and ties keep ballot order. Voting-model and valid-vote counts show how much evidence supports each score; a high score based on few votes is less informative. Changing the model cohort recalculates the ranking.
