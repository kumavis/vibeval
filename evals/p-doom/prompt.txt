TARGET
Independently research and construct a probabilistic model for the unconditional chance of a global AI-related catastrophe by the LEAP resolution date of December 31, 2050. Use the LEAP Wave 9 event and resolution criteria, summarized here:
- A qualifying AI-caused event kills MORE THAN 10% of the people alive at the start of a relevant five-year period by the end of that period. The denominator is that start-of-period population; do not substitute a net population decline or count unborn people.
- The five-year period must begin on or after December 31, 2025 and end BEFORE the December 31, 2050 resolution date. An initiating incident before 2050 is insufficient if its qualifying fatality period ends too late.
- AI is the direct or proximate cause. Proximate cause uses a but-for test: absent substantial AI involvement within the year before the event, the event would not occur or would be extremely unlikely. Attributable subsequent consequences count. Incidental AI assistance that does not materially enable the event does not qualify.
- AI includes machines performing sophisticated information processing; no AGI or superintelligence prerequisite is stipulated.
- Resolution uses broadly recognized population/fatality data, with relevant expert panels for disagreements that affect resolution.
Reference: https://leap.forecastingresearch.org/reports/wave9#ai-catastrophic-risk
Forecast unconditionally on progress, capability arrival and deployment, using information available at the supplied as-of timestamp. Do not answer a slow-, moderate- or rapid-progress conditional question, literal-extinction question.

RESEARCH
Use live web search and open primary sources. Search for risk-increasing and risk-reducing evidence. Prefer a few relevant, directly read sources to extra citations. Check whether newer studies supersede older model evaluations. Distinguish observed capability from projected capability and a plausible mechanism from demonstrated real-world success. Stay at the level of risk analysis; operational instructions for causing harm are unnecessary.

For surveys, record the actual outcome, time horizon, conditioning, participant selection and question wording. Do not copy a headline p(doom) or average incompatible questions. For empirical studies, preserve the tested system, task, result, limitations, and any mitigations that were actually developed or deployed. A reported vulnerability followed by a successful patch is evidence about both risk and response. Do not omit the latter. Describe food shortages, modeled deaths and observed deaths accurately; none automatically establishes the LEAP fatality outcome caused by AI.

Supply 6–16 distinct sources across at least three hostnames, including primary research and at least one source supporting a counterargument. Source observations should say what the evidence supports; probability parameters are judgments informed by that evidence, not measured facts. Source counts and fluent citations do not verify truth.

MODEL
Build your own compact positive, acyclic causal graph with 2–20 independent Bernoulli parameter switches and 1–32 deterministic rules. Use lowercase atom identifiers with digits/underscores, starting with a letter; no spaces, variables, negation, recursion, external predicates or free Prolog code. Parameter atoms must be unique and cannot also be rule heads. Reserve derived head p_doom for the target event. Every atom must contribute to p_doom. Do not repeat a rule or repeat an atom inside a rule body.

Every parameter requires an event definition, low/central/high probabilities as FRACTIONS with 0 <= low <= central <= high <= 1, sourceIds referencing the supplied sources, and a rationale. Explain why the central value and endpoints are plausible, what observations inform them, where judgment fills an evidence gap, and what event the probability is conditional on. An unconditional switch should explicitly say so. If the evidence does not identify a numerical probability, acknowledge that rather than implying it does.

A parameter is an independent random switch. A conditional outcome switch must NOT already include the probability of its prerequisite: the deterministic rule will combine both. A rule body is logical AND; multiple rules for a head are logical OR. Reuse the SAME prerequisite atom across correlated routes rather than independent copies, and do not simply add route probabilities. Explain remaining switch independence and shared causes in dependencies. Choose prerequisites only when your mechanism requires them; a common capability or deployment gate is not mandatory. Justify your causal decomposition and major omitted possibilities without following a prescribed pathway taxonomy. Distinguish an initiating hazard from the additional conditions needed to cross the fatality threshold within a qualifying five-year window.

Explain omitted mechanisms, evidence gaps, the strongest sourced counterarguments, and at least one plausible alternative graph structure. Explain why broader survey outcomes do or do not transfer to this target. Low/high values are assumption scenarios, not calibrated confidence intervals. Parameter sensitivity holds the graph fixed and does not measure structural uncertainty. Derive the final probability using the harness's ProbLog result rather than inventing or manually overriding it. There is no desired probability and no ground-truth accuracy score for an unresolved 2050 event.
